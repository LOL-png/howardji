About

Hi, I'm Howard! I'm a student at Stanford University studying robotics and artificial intelligence.

I'm passionate about developing intelligent robotic systems that can meaningfully interact with and assist humans. My research spans robot learning, computer vision, and medical imaging applications.

Currently, I'm working on a generative robot learning policy at the Stanford Vision and Learning Lab. Previously, I worked on collaborative human-robot manipulation at the CHARM Lab.

In the past, I've developed a column-climbing robot for infrastructure inspection that earned me recognition as a 2024 Regeneron Science Talent Search Finalist. I've also published research on biological oscillator modeling in the PUMP Journal.

In my free time, I enjoy building hardware projects, participating in the Stanford Space Initiative, and exploring new applications of machine learning in robotics.

Research

Generative Robot Learning Model
Stanford Vision and Learning Lab (Prof. Jiajun Wu, Dr. Ruoshi Huang, Yunzhi Zhang), Stanford (June 2025 - Present)
Designed and implemented a predictive robot action model using vision-language models (VLMs), combining generative flow tracking, video-based representation learning, and action reward. Trained and deployed models in simulation and on a physical Franka R3 arm to demonstrate sim-to-real transfer.
DaVinci Surgical Three-Hand Robot Learning
Collaborative Haptics and Robotics in Medicine Lab (Prof. Allison Okamura, Dr. Alaa Eldin Abdelaal), Stanford (June 2025 - December 2025)
Developed a robot action model using Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) for collaborative human-robot manipulation tasks with the DaVinci surgical system. Integrated ACT into real-world collaborative workflows via custom ROS nodes and Isaac Sim for training and evaluation.
Column-climbing Robot for Real-time Bridge Inspection
Independent Project (February 2022 - May 2023)
Developed an autonomous multi-robot system capable of climbing columns and mapping surface cracks in real-time. Achieved 82.6% mean average precision while climbing at 4.7 cm/s on real bridges.
Published at IEEE GlobConET Conference • 2024 Regeneron Science Talent Search Finalist
Stochastic and Deterministic Models of Biological Oscillators
Read Lab, UC Irvine (June 2022 - August 2023)
Compared computational models of oscillatory genetic circuits involving repressor proteins using periodograms, heat maps, and peak-finding techniques.
First-author publication in PUMP Journal
