About

Hi, I'm Howard! I'm a student at Stanford University studying robotics and artificial intelligence.

I'm passionate about developing intelligent robotic systems that can meaningfully interact with and assist humans. My research spans robot learning, computer vision, and medical imaging applications.

Currently, I'm working on physics-aware video generation for robotics at the Stanford Geometric Computation Group (Guibas Lab). Previously, I worked on a generative robot learning policy at the Stanford Vision and Learning Lab, and on collaborative human-robot manipulation at the CHARM Lab.

In the past, I've developed a column-climbing robot for infrastructure inspection that earned me recognition as a 2024 Regeneron Science Talent Search Finalist.

In my free time, I enjoy building hardware projects and competing in hackathons. I'm especially interested in vision-language-action models (VLAs), world models, and reinforcement learning for robotics.

Research

Physics-Aware Video Generation for Robotics
Stanford Geometric Computation Group (Prof. Leonidas Guibas, Yang Zheng, Baoyang Deng), Stanford (June 2026 - Present)
Designing a physics-aware video generation framework for robotics that jointly predicts RGB frames and physical states, drawing on video diffusion, world models, and inverse-dynamics.
TrAct: Bridging Robot Control and Visual Prediction with Visual Tracks
Stanford Vision and Learning Lab (Prof. Jiajun Wu, Dr. Raven Huang, Yunzhi Zhang), Stanford (June 2025 - June 2026)
Built TrAct, a robot decision framework that uses visual tracks to link a VLA policy, a track-conditioned video diffusion world model, and a VLM reward model. Improved task success from 27% to 55% in simulation and 49% to 76% on a real Franka arm, and demonstrated sim-to-real transfer and cross-embodiment generalization (Franka → UR5).
Co-first author, submitted to CoRL
DaVinci Surgical Three-Hand Robot Learning
Collaborative Haptics and Robotics in Medicine Lab (Prof. Allison Okamura, Dr. Alaa Eldin Abdelaal), Stanford (June 2025 - December 2025)
Developed a robot action model using Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) for collaborative human-robot manipulation tasks with the DaVinci surgical system. Integrated ACT into real-world collaborative workflows via custom ROS nodes and Isaac Sim for training and evaluation.
Column-climbing Robot for Real-time Bridge Inspection
Independent Project (February 2022 - May 2023)
Developed an autonomous multi-robot system capable of climbing columns and mapping surface cracks in real-time. Achieved 82.6% mean average precision while climbing at 4.7 cm/s on real bridges.
Published at IEEE GlobConET Conference • 2024 Regeneron Science Talent Search Finalist • Paper
